---
title: "Early-Season Overreactions Worth Having (and Ones to Ignore)"
categories: [analysis]
---


One week of data is almost nothing, and yet it's exactly when fantasy managers make their most dramatic roster moves. Some of that instinct is right. Most of it isn't.

## Overreactions worth having

A rookie who saw an unexpectedly high snap share in Week 1 is worth believing in — offensive coordinators don't hand out big roles by accident in the opener. Similarly, a veteran who suddenly looks a step slower deserves real concern, not just a bad-week excuse.

## Overreactions to ignore

Touchdown regression is real. A player who scored twice on limited touches isn't a locked-in weekly starter yet — check the underlying opportunity (targets, carries, red zone looks) before buying in.

## The actual takeaway

Weight opportunity over outcome for the first two or three weeks. Volume is sticky; touchdowns are noisy.
