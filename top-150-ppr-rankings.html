Top 150 fantasy football rankings for PPR deep leagues

Rest-of-season values, full PPR, one starting quarterback. Current through Week 7 (October 20, 2026).

This is the list for managers who need to know who is actually worth a roster spot when every team has 16 or more players to fill. Each player is ranked by what he should be worth from here through the fantasy playoffs in a full-point-per-reception league, then grouped into tiers so you can see where the real drop-offs are. Use the tiers when you are trading or choosing between two players: moving between names in the same tier rarely matters, and moving across a tier line usually does.

The list has 52 running backs, 59 wide receivers, 23 tight ends, and 16 quarterbacks. Quarterbacks sit lower than their raw points suggest because only one starts in most leagues. For this week's lineup decisions, pair the list with the PPR flex rankings for deep leagues, and for roster-building see the guide to 16-team fantasy football waiver wire strategy. How this list was built

Every rank on this page was produced by an AI model for a class project, and it is an opinion, not a projection. It starts from two public sources, both published on October 20, 2026:

  • The Week 7 trade-value chart and rest-of-season rankings from CBS Sports (Dave Richard), which publishes separate values for full-PPR leagues.
  • The Week 7 rest-of-season consensus rankings from USA Today's fantasy staff, as carried on Yahoo Sports. These are built for half-PPR leagues, so they are adjusted below.

Positions and rest-of-season position lists were cross-checked against a third public set of rankings (Wolf Sports). Where a player appears in only one source, that source sets his place.

Adjustments for PPR and deep leagues

  • Blend. A player's two ranks are combined, with the 12-team consensus counting for 60 percent and the PPR trade values for 40 percent.
  • PPR bump. Running backs move up about 3 percent and wide receivers and tight ends about 2 percent, because a catch is worth a full point and the half-PPR source undercounts them.
  • Deep-league quarterback discount. Quarterbacks move down about 12 percent. In a 14- or 16-team league with one starting quarterback, a replacement is close to free relative to the points he scores, while a replacement running back or receiver is not.

Tiers are fixed rank bands (the first six players, then the next nine, and so on), not statistical clusters. They are there to help you see where depth thins out, not to claim precision.

Read this before you trade

Rosters, roles, and injuries change daily. Check each player's status before you act, and treat anyone listed as questionable or out as a different player until he is back. Rookies and players with new teams carry the widest range of outcomes. The list is refreshed each week during the season.
