In a 16-team fantasy football league the waiver wire is not a place to find bargains. It is an auction where every manager is bidding on the same four or five players, and the winner is usually the manager who decided what each player was worth before the bidding opened. This post is a plan for making that decision in advance.

## Know what the wire actually holds

In a deep league the free-agent pool is mostly roles, not talent. The players worth bidding on are backup running backs behind fragile starters, third receivers who just moved into the lineup, and one-week fill-ins for teams on bye. Almost nobody on the wire is a breakout star, so the goal is to buy the clearest role at a fair price.

## A bid ladder

Assume a $100 budget and scale the percentages to your league's. These ranges are this site's opinion, not a formula.

| Tier | Typical bid | Who belongs here |
|---|---|---|
| Starter with a clear role | 15 to 25% | A running back who just inherited the lead job |
| Weekly flex or bye fill-in | 5 to 10% | A receiver with a steady target share |
| Handcuff to a fragile starter | 3 to 8% | A backup running back |
| Depth and insurance | 1 to 3% | A streaming tight end or backup quarterback |

## Four rules

1. **Buy roles, not box scores.** A player who scored twice on four touches is a bet on touchdowns. A player who got sixteen touches is a bet on a role. Roles repeat, and touchdowns do not.
2. **Handcuff before you chase a stranger.** If you own a running back who has a history of injuries or a heavy workload, the backup behind him is the most valuable player on the wire for you. Bid a little now, because the bid after the injury will be ten times larger.
3. **Keep money for the second half.** Injuries pile up after midseason and the best roles open then. Try to carry at least a third of your budget past Week 8.
4. **Bid in odd numbers.** If everyone rounds to $10, a bid of $11 or $13 breaks ties without costing much more.

## If your league uses waiver priority instead

Some leagues skip the budget and use a rolling priority order. The same logic applies, with a different currency. Spend your priority only on a role change, such as a starter's injury or a new lead back, and never on a streamer. A priority spent on a one-week tight end is gone when the real opportunity shows up.
