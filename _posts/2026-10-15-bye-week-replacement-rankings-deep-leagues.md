Four teams are on bye in each of the next two weeks. With 32 teams in the league, four byes take about one team in eight out of action, so a lineup with seven starting spots loses a little under one starter to a bye in a typical week. In a 16-team league, where the wire is thin, that one hole can cost you the week. This post ranks the best fantasy football bye week replacements, in order.

## The next two byes

| Week | Teams on bye |
|---|---|
| 6 | Bengals, Lions, Dolphins, Vikings |
| 7 | Bills, Jaguars, Chargers, Commanders |

The schedule is published by the league, but check your league host to confirm it before you plan around it.

## Ranking your options

Fill the hole in this order. The order matters, because each step is riskier or costlier than the one before it.

1. **A bench player with a defined role at the same position.** You already own him, he costs nothing, and you know what he does. If you have one, use him and stop.
2. **A waiver add with a defined role.** If your bench has nobody, the next best move is a player with a clear role from the wire. See [deep league waiver wire pickups]({{ '/rankings/week-6-deep-league-waiver-wire-adds/' | relative_url }}) for the options.
3. **A matchup stream.** At tight end and defense, you can often stream a favorable opponent. See [fantasy football streaming rankings]({{ '/rankings/week-6-streaming-rankings-deep-leagues/' | relative_url }}).
4. **A flex swap.** If nothing fits, move a different position into the empty slot. Flex spots are flexible for a reason, and a running back in the flex can free a receiver slot for a better pickup.

## Replacement options by position this week

- **Quarterback:** Jameis Winston of the Giants is the best deep-league fill-in, coming off a top-six week. He is a one-week play, not a long-term answer.
- **Wide receiver:** Germie Bernard of the Steelers and Dontayvion Wicks of the Eagles have the clearest recent roles.
- **Tight end:** Pat Freiermuth, Brenton Strange, and Hunter Henry cover the position, in that order.
- **Running back:** Samaje Perine is a Week 7 stash because the Bengals are off this week.

## Plan two weeks ahead

The managers who struggle on bye weeks are the ones who shop on Tuesday, when everyone else is shopping too. By Week 5 you should already know which starters sit in Weeks 6 through 9, and you should stash replacements before the week they are needed.

One caution about the Steelers: both Bernard and Freiermuth are Steelers, and Pittsburgh has a bye in Week 9. They solve a bye-week problem now and create one in a few weeks, so plan the next replacement before you need it.
