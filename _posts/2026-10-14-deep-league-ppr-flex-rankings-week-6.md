In a deep league the flex spot is really an availability contest. Everyone has the same four or five names to choose from, so the question is not who has the highest ceiling but whose role you can trust. These Week 6 flex rankings are for PPR leagues with 14 or more teams, and they are ordered by confidence first and upside second. Information is current through Wednesday, October 14.

## The rankings

1. **Woody Marks, RB, Texans.** Marks handled 19 touches against Tennessee last week while David Montgomery struggled and then left with a groin injury, and reports list Montgomery as expected to miss this week. If that holds, Marks is the lead back for a road game in Jacksonville. A role this big is a start whatever the matchup.
2. **Dontayvion Wicks, WR, Eagles.** Wicks has reached double figures in three of five games and has a favorable-looking matchup against a Carolina secondary described as missing both starting corners. He is a safer flex than most receivers because his role has already shown up in the box score.
3. **Germie Bernard, WR, Steelers.** Bernard's six catches last week came on nine targets, which is the kind of target share that carries a PPR flex. The risk is that his role depends on Roman Wilson staying out.
4. **Kendrick Bourne, WR, Cardinals.** Bourne saw eight targets in Week 5 as Arizona's third passing option. He is a floor play, a way to fill the spot without gambling, and I would not expect a big week.
5. **Emmett Johnson, RB, Chiefs.** Johnson is a handcuff, and handcuffs are not flex plays until the starter is hurt. Start him only if Kenneth Walker III is ruled out. Otherwise he belongs on your bench.

## How to use these rankings

Check inactives about ninety minutes before kickoff, because three of these calls depend on a teammate's status. If Montgomery plays, move Marks down a spot. If Wilson plays, move Bernard down. Flex decisions in a 16-team league are best made by asking which player you would regret not starting, and the answer is usually the one with the clearest role.
